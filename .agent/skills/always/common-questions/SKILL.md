description: How to answer the questions many forms ask, such as salary, start date, sponsorship and notice period.
---
Questions most forms ask, and what to answer when the answers give nothing more precise. The answers always win over this.

- Salary expectations: the answers' `desired_salary` when there is one; otherwise "Negotiable" in a text field, and in a number field the lowest number the form accepts only when it is required. Never "Please see my attached CV" for a salary.
- Start date or availability: the answers' `earliest_start_date`; a date field takes the first day of that month.
- Notice period: the answers' `notice_period`; a student or someone between roles says "Available immediately" only when the answers say so, else leave an optional one empty.
- Willing to relocate, work mode (remote, hybrid, on-site): the answers' `willing_to_relocate` and `work_mode`.
- Work authorization and sponsorship: `authorized_to_work_in_us`, `requires_visa_sponsorship`, `visa_type`, exactly as the answers say. Never guess these: a required one the answers leave out stops the form as missing information.
- "How did you hear about us": the answers' `how_did_you_hear`, else the option closest to a company careers page or a job board.
- Graduation date and GPA: from the education in the answers; a degree in progress graduates at its end date.
- Years of experience: count from the CV's dates up to today, each month once even where roles overlap, and never round up. Internships and part-time roles during school are what a student has: count them at their real length (a year and a half is 1, or "1.5" where decimals are allowed), and pick the lowest band that holds that number.
