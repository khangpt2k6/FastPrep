---
name: Two stories, two numbers
description: Show the fit with two pieces of real work, each told as a short story and each carrying one number the reader will remember.
order: 2
---
Read the posting first and find what it needs most: usually the first few requirements and the work it describes for the
first months. Then find where the candidate has done that kind of work.

- Tell two pieces of work, not ten. A letter that lists everything proves nothing. The first is the one closest to this
  role's work and gets the most room. The second shows another side: leading, teaching, working with people.
- When the candidate's own stories are given (a rule named "My stories"), tell those, in their facts. Pick the two that
  fit this role best.
- Each story has the same bones: what was asked or what was wrong, the hard moment, what the candidate did about it,
  what came of it, and what they learned from it.
- Each story carries exactly one signature number, the one that shows what the work meant for people: records handled,
  people served, hours saved, events led. Two such numbers in the whole letter. A third figure only when a story cannot
  be told without it, and never more than three.
- Choose the number a recruiter would repeat to a colleague: how many people, records, hours or events. A figure only
  an engineer can weigh (a latency, a throughput) is not one.
- Copy each number as the CV or the story writes it, with its "more than" or its "+".
- Say how the past work maps to this job's work in plain words. Do not restate the CV line by line: the CV is attached.
- One idea per sentence. A sentence that needs "and" twice is two sentences.
