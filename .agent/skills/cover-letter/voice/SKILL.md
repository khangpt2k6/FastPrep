---
name: Sound like a person
description: Plain, natural wording in short sentences. No AI tells, no clichés, no dashes, no labels glued to sentences.
order: 4
---
Write the way a capable person writes a short note to someone they respect: direct, warm, a little informal, never stiff.

- Short sentences. Most under 20 words. Vary the length a little so it does not read like a list.
- First person, active voice. "I built", not "was responsible for building".
- Never use an en dash or an em dash. Use a comma, a period, parentheses, or a hyphen with spaces ( - ) instead.
- Never glue a label to the front of a sentence: no "What:", "Why:", "How:", "The why", "Here's the thing",
  "What excited me about", "What I love about", "What this means for you". Just say the thing.
- No stock openers or closers: "I am writing to express my interest", "I am excited to apply", "Please find attached",
  "I believe I would be a great fit", "Thank you for your time and consideration" as the whole closing.
- Do not start the letter with "I am applying" or "My name is". Open with the substance, the strongest match, and name
  the role in the same or the next sentence.
- No comma splices: two complete sentences joined by a comma are two sentences.
- No buzzwords or AI tells: passionate, synergy, leverage, dynamic, fast-paced environment, team player, hit the ground
  running, results-driven, detail-oriented, thrilled, delve, tapestry, testament, furthermore, moreover, in today's world.
- No exclamation marks. No emojis. No rhetorical questions.
- Confident, not boastful: state what was done and let the result speak. No "I am the perfect candidate".
