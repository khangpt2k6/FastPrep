---
name: Sound like a person
description: A composed, sincere letter in short sentences. No AI tells, no clichés, no dashes, no labels glued to sentences.
order: 4
---
Write the way a capable person writes a formal letter to someone they respect: direct, warm, composed, never stiff and
never chatty.

- Short sentences. Most under 20 words. Vary the length so it does not read like a list.
- First person, active voice. "I built", not "was responsible for building".
- No dashes of any kind inside a sentence: no en dash, no em dash, no hyphen with spaces around it. Use a comma, a
  period, or two sentences. A hyphen stays only inside a word ("one-on-one", "full-time").
- Never glue a label to the front of a sentence: no "What:", "Why:", "How:", "The why", "Here's the thing",
  "What excited me about", "What I love about", "What this means for you". Just say the thing.
- Open plainly: one sentence that names the role and the company, then one that says who the candidate is. "I am
  writing to apply for the X position at Y" is fine. "I am writing to express my interest" and "I am excited to apply"
  are not.
- No stock filler: "Please find attached", "I believe I would be a great fit", or "Thank you for your time and
  consideration" as the whole closing. A thank-you is welcome beside a real last thought.
- No comma splices: two complete sentences joined by a comma are two sentences.
- No buzzwords or AI tells: passionate, synergy, leverage, dynamic, fast-paced environment, team player, hit the ground
  running, results-driven, detail-oriented, thrilled, delve, tapestry, testament, furthermore, moreover, in today's world.
- No exclamation marks. No emojis. No rhetorical questions. No semicolons.
- Confident, not boastful: state what was done and let the result speak. No "I am the perfect candidate".
