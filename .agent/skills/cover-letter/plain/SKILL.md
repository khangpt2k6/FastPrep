---
name: Keep it light on tech
description: Write for a recruiter, not an engineer. Few tool names, no jargon, impact and motivation in plain words.
order: 5
---
The first reader is usually a recruiter or a hiring manager skimming many letters. The CV already lists the stack.

- At most two tool, language or framework names in the whole letter, and only the ones the posting asks for most.
- No jargon or architecture talk: no "microservices", "CI/CD pipelines", "REST endpoints", "latency", "scalable
  infrastructure" and the like. Say what the work did for people instead: "made the app load twice as fast for users".
- Describe each project by its purpose and its result, not by how it was built. One plain sentence a non-engineer can
  follow beats three precise technical ones.
- Spend more of the letter on why this role and this team, and on how the candidate works and learns, than on tools.
- Numbers are welcome when they show impact (users, time saved, growth). Skip numbers that only an engineer cares about.
- When the posting itself is very technical, still keep the letter readable: name the one or two skills it stresses most
  and explain the rest through results.
