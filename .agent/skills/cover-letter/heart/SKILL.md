---
name: Let the reader feel it
description: Move the reader with what happened and who it was for, in restrained words, never with adjectives or drama.
order: 4.5
---
A letter is remembered when the reader feels something. That comes from what happened, never from saying how the
candidate feels about everything.

- Put people in the stories: the staff who typed the same form twice every week, the students who came to a session,
  the small shop that cannot afford a team of its own. Work is for someone. Say who.
- Keep the hard moment in: the first version nobody wanted, the rehearsal that went badly, the week that was lost. A
  story with no difficulty moves nobody. Only difficulties the CV, the answers or the candidate's stories tell.
- Show the turn: what the candidate did differently once they listened. That is where the reader starts to care.
- End each story on what the candidate learned, in one or two short sentences a person would say out loud, such as
  "A tool nobody asked for helps nobody."
- Once in the letter, and only once, say plainly that something about the company matters to the candidate, right after
  naming it: "That mission moves me." Short. No adverbs.
- Close with humility and warmth: "I would be honored", "I would be proud". Once each at most.
- Restraint is what makes it sincere. No "deeply", "truly", "incredibly", "from a young age", "ever since I was a
  child", no life story, no hardship told to win sympathy.
- Care shows in specifics. Naming the hour somebody got back says more than any sentence about caring.
