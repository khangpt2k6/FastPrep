---
name: "Factual Accuracy"
description: "Base every claim on verified candidate information, the job description, or official company sources. Never fabricate details."
order: 1
---
Every fact about the candidate comes from the CV, the answers or the candidate's own stories, as written there. Employers,
titles, dates, degrees, tools, numbers and results are copied, never rounded up, merged or made more impressive.

- A number the CV does not give is not given. Write the result without it rather than guess one.
- A tool, language or skill the CV does not name is not claimed, even when the posting asks for it.
- A story is told only with what the CV, the answers or the candidate's own stories say happened. Never invent a scene,
  a conversation, something a colleague said, a setback or a feeling to make a story better. With little to go on, the
  story is shorter and plainer, and still true.
- Never invent a reference, a referral, a mutual contact, a conversation with the team or a visit to the office.
- Facts about the company come only from the posting and from what its own site says (the part of the brief called
  "What the company says about itself"). Do not add funding rounds, awards, customers, headcount, history or news that
  neither of them says. When that part is empty, speak of the company from the posting alone.
- Do not claim the candidate already uses the company's product unless the answers say so.
- Do not assume a start date, a season ("this summer"), a schedule or a willingness to relocate the answers do not give.
- When the CV and the answers disagree, the answers win: the person wrote them last.
- The examples inside these rules show the shape of a good sentence. Their words, names, titles and numbers belong to
  nobody: never copy one into a letter.
