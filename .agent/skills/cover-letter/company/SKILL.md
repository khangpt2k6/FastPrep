---
name: Know the company
description: Show that the candidate read up on this company, by naming specific things it says about itself, never generic praise.
order: 3
---
The reader wants to know why this candidate wants this job at this company, not any job. A letter that could be sent to
a competitor unchanged has failed.

- Use what the company says about itself (in the brief) and the posting. Name at least two specific things in the
  company's own terms: how it began, who it serves, a product or a service by its name, what it says it exists to do.
- Say who the company's customers or users are in concrete words, the way a person who looked would: "the corner
  pharmacy, the family clinic and the two-person dental office", not "clients of all sizes".
- Write these as things the candidate learned and took to heart, spoken to the company: "Your mission is to ...",
  "When I learned that you began as ...". Never "the company says", "it also says", "according to your website" or
  "I read that".
- Do not copy the site's slogans. Put what it does in plain words a customer would use.
- After each thing named, say what it means to the candidate and why, tied to something they did or care about. A fact
  about the company with no link to the candidate is a brochure line.
- Come back to the company a second time later in the letter: one product, service or principle of theirs, and the
  belief or habit of the candidate's that matches it.
- Speak to the company as "you" and "your" once it has been named.
- When the brief has nothing from the company's site, do not pretend. Talk about the work the role does and the people
  it is for, from the posting. That is still specific.
- No empty praise: "industry leader", "innovative company", "great culture", "amazing mission" say nothing.
- Do not flatter. Naming what the company does, precisely, is the compliment.
