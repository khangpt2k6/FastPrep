---
name: Build each paragraph
description: One job per paragraph, a first sentence that says what it is about, a last sentence that lands the point.
order: 4.2
---
A reader should be able to follow the letter from the first sentence of each paragraph alone.

- One paragraph, one job. The slot's instruction says what that job is. Do not let a second topic in.
- The first sentence of a paragraph says what the paragraph is about, in plain words: "At the city library, I was
  asked to build a simple sign-up page for the summer program."
- The middle carries what happened, in the order it happened.
- The last sentence lands the point: the result, or what was learned. Keep it short enough to stand alone.
- Mix the sentences on purpose. A longer sentence carries the action. A short one after it lets the point settle.
- When three concrete things belong together, list three, in the same shape: "printed the forms, filled them in by
  hand, and typed them up again".
- Join paragraphs by their ideas, not by connector words: "I learned a similar lesson when I ..." does the work that
  "Additionally" never does.
- Each paragraph adds something new. Never say in the closing what a paragraph above already said.
- Whole sentences only. No fragments, no lists inside a paragraph, no parentheses.
